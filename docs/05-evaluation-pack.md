## 1. Prepare a real evaluation

1. Identify an adult stakeholder relevant to your own professional, learning or research context: e.g., a Hong Kong university learner, English support teacher or educational technology practitioner. Explain why their perspective matters; do not claim they represent all stakeholders.
2. Check course/institutional procedures for consent, recruitment and use of AI. Follow any instructor-specific instructions. Do not invent an ethics approval number or assume course permission to use AI is permission to collect personal data.
3. Use a clean local demo profile with no real third-party details. Show that deterministic mode is scripted. If demonstrating live AI, explain its provider separately; do not send participant interview content to a model without an applicable permission and approved procedure.
4. Agree a location, interview language and approximately 25–30 minutes. Participation must be voluntary, with no effect on grades or access to support. Avoid recruiting someone over whom you hold authority where refusal may feel difficult.
5. Explain that the plan, multilingual note, check-ins, self-review and support plan stay on the local server, while text deliberately sent in a live practice can reach the AI provider. The plan may be private from the model but is not encrypted against filesystem access. Avoid identifiable examples. A support-plan status is a self-report, not a confirmed appointment.
6. Store signed consent separately from pseudonymized notes. Complete the retention/deletion date before recruitment. Prefer written notes; any interview recording is a separate research decision and is never performed through SpeakUp.

## 4. Walkthrough and semi-structured interview guide

### Introduction (3 minutes)

Explain prototype scope, demo limitations and voluntary participation. Confirm consent and preferred language. Ask: “In what situations do university students in your context choose to use English, and when might another language make more sense?” Avoid assuming every student is anxious or needs more English.

### Optional walkthrough (6 minutes)

Use a fresh pseudonymous session and select a task relevant to the participant, such as asking for feedback on reasoning or clarifying a lecture point. Choose three short tasks below to fit the agreed time; do not rush through every feature. Ask for think-aloud comments and record any help given. A written intention, an AI turn and a hypothetical plan are software actions, not human participation. Leave human and support-contact statuses at “not yet”/“planned” unless contact really occurred.

| Optional usability task | Observable evidence | Neutral prompt |
|---|---|---|
| Choose a scenario and inspect its communicative move | Selection, misinterpretation, request for explanation | “Which part fits your course, and what would differ?” |
| Save a private plan with optional multilingual note/check-in | Completion, errors, whether data destination is understood | “What do you expect the AI to receive?” |
| Show/hide hints and choose support | Control found, assistance, preference and reason | “When would you want a hint available?” |
| Send one practice turn; inspect a quoted suggestion; make a self-review | Quote understood, suggestion accepted/questioned, useful next try | “What would you keep, adapt or check with someone?” |
| Prepare an opening, timing and exit for a human task | Plan usability, unwanted pressure, suitable opportunity | “How could you choose to stop or listen instead?” |
| Create a support question and inspect an official resource | Appropriate role, eligibility/booking uncertainty, follow-up understood | “Would this route be usable in your situation?” |

Log only actual observations: task attempted, completed without help/with help/not completed, interaction problem, assistance and a brief source note. Time a task only if timing was really captured; with one participant use the observed time, not an inferred population average. Do not describe opening a service page or saving a plan as receiving support. The app does not book appointments or contact people.

### Interview (15–18 minutes)

1. **Academic relevance and expectations:** Which scenario fits a real course task, and which expectation would need changing? Does the writing-feedback task help you formulate a useful question, or does it miss your writing needs?
2. **Participation and opportunity:** When would you choose to listen? When might you want to contribute but lack space? What would help distinguish those situations without judging a learner's ability?
3. **Strategies and self-review:** Is the intention/strategy useful or extra work? Which hint would you show or hide? What would make a takeaway change your next action?
4. **AI feedback and judgment:** Is the quoted evidence accurate? Does the alternative preserve the intended meaning and respect your English? What needs teacher or disciplinary checking? Does quotation checking risk making uncertain advice look authoritative?
5. **Human transfer and autonomy:** What, if anything, could carry from rehearsal to a person? Does planning an opening, moment and exit feel helpful or controlling? Could AI become an alternative to approaching people?
6. **Help-seeking and follow-up:** Who would be appropriate for your question? Did you know about the service, can you use it, and how would you judge whether advice helped? What eligibility, disciplinary fit, scheduling or language barriers remain?
7. **Feasibility, inclusion and privacy:** What time, accessibility, browser or campus constraints matter? Explain which fields stay local and what can reach an AI or browser speech service. What would you avoid entering?
8. **Refinement and evidence:** Which one feature should change first, why, and what observation would convince you the revision helped? What could this short walkthrough never establish?

Neutral probes: “Can you give an example?” “Was there a part that did not fit?” “What makes you say that?” Avoid “How much did this improve your confidence?” as it presupposes an improvement. Do not treat a request to clarify an instruction, a multilingual note or intentional listening as evidence of poor language ability.

### Close (2 minutes)

Summarize the participant’s key points and invite correction. Confirm quotation permission and withdrawal deadline. Thank them without implying endorsement. Record researcher reflections immediately afterward, separate from participant statements.

## 5. Evaluation framework

| Question | Evidence to collect | Analysis and boundary | Possible response, not a finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Is the academic task relevant? | A real task, course expectation and counterexample | Context/discipline fit; one view cannot establish prevalence | Rewrite move, role or example with teacher input |
| Does the plan support a chosen strategy? | Actual use/nonuse of intention, hints and self-review; reason | Strategy awareness is not verified strategy effectiveness | Simplify prompts, make a hint more optional |
| Is AI coaching actionable and contestable? | Exact quote, learner interpretation, disputed rewrite, possible meaning change | Attribution check is separate from semantic quality | Revise prompt or add a teacher-check route |
| Is a human step plausible and voluntary? | Opportunity, lack of space, chosen listening, exit option | Intention and simulated success are not human behavior | Smaller optional task or alternative participation |
| Can support be found and used? | Awareness, eligibility, access, question fit, potential follow-up | Saving a plan is not contact; contact is not useful support | Clarify official resource and appropriate role |
| Is autonomy retained? | Contrary comments on targets, ratings and challenge | No automatic inference from confidence or silence | Reduce burden or prominence of counts |
| Can the interface be used? | Real task completion, errors, time if captured and assistance | Descriptive usability observations, not a learning-effect test | Improve labels, focus or mobile layout |
| Is privacy understood? | Participant explanation of local/private versus remote fields | Comprehension, not a security certification | Rewrite notice or field labels |
| Is a trial feasible and inclusive? | Actual device, time, opportunity and service constraints | Local feasibility, not general adoption | Text-first access and flexible task alternatives |

Use a small evidence table after the real interview:

| Evidence ID | Anonymized quote/paraphrase + source location | Interpretation | Literature relationship | Design decision | Contrary evidence / uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [E1] | [real note line or transcript timestamp] | [your analysis] | [support, tension, or extension] | [specific revision] | [alternative explanation] |

Suggested process: read notes; mark candidate codes (e.g., academic expectation, strategy burden, opportunity, intentional listening, feedback trust, support access, follow-up, privacy); allow unexpected codes; group into 2–3 themes; compare each with literature; consider negative cases; propose a revision tied to evidence. This is a transparent small-scale analysis, not a claim of saturation or inter-rater reliability. A single researcher should acknowledge interpretive bias and any relationship to the participant.

## 6. Optional later pilot — exploratory evidence, not the interview assignment

A later short pilot can investigate feasibility before an outcome study. It does not replace the required real interview or authorize new data collection. Agree a relevant task, consent and data plan first. Keep three evidence streams separate:

| Stream | Suitable records | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Software usability | Observed tasks, assistance, failures and feedback inspected | Educational improvement |
| Personal task experience | Optional same-scenario before/after check-ins, strategy used, chosen next action | Validated WTC change, proficiency or causality |
| Human/support follow-up | Voluntary self-reported attempt, opportunity, intentional listening, advice sought/adapted and usefulness | Independently verified behavior or guaranteed support benefit |

An exploratory schedule might collect a baseline task description, a short initial walkthrough and one or two agreed follow-ups. Offer an explicit option to report no opportunity or chosen listening. Record missing responses, withdrawal, scenario changes and actual human support received. Use no public ranking or reward for contacting someone, and require neither third-party recording nor names. Keep human reports distinct from AI-turn counts.

If both personal check-ins exist, show each participant's paired ratings in the same scenario with dates and context. Do not pool different scenarios into a claimed gain or infer improvement from the latest profile setting. Repeated practice, task familiarity, existing support, opportunity, expectation and selective completion may explain a difference. Without an appropriate comparison, such patterns are descriptive and hypothesis-generating.

For a later study claiming a WTC or other construct outcome, select an instrument that is validated for the intended construct, language, population and use. Read the original development/validation work, check use conditions, and seek appropriate methodological advice. Do not shorten, translate, combine or change items and continue calling the result validated. The app's optional single-item ratings remain personal check-ins. An adequately designed comparison, proportionate ethics process and account of confounding/attrition would still be needed for a causal claim.

Use the literature critically. Shi's strategy correlations, Wang et al.'s cross-sectional statistical mediation and participant accounts of useful feedback motivate questions; they are not intervention effects. A larger sample or a meta-analysis does not remove design bias. The source-specific corrections and limits are recorded in [the literature evidence matrix](08-literature-evidence-matrix.md).
